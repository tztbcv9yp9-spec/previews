It heats as well as cools

Modern air conditioning is a heat pump. It moves heat instead of making it, so the unit that cools a bedroom in July warms it in January. There is no second heater to buy or store.

Running costs depend on the model and how you use it, so ask for the maker's figures for the unit we quote.

Why the F-Gas certificate matters

Whoever you choose, ask for their certificate number before work starts.

It is the law

Air conditioning holds refrigerant gas. The firm and the engineer both have to be certified to fit or service it.

It protects the warranty

Makers can refuse a warranty claim on a system that was not fitted by a certified installer.

You get the paperwork

A commissioning record and the warranty registration, handed over on the day.

Questions people ask first

What will it cost?

The table above shows where prices start. Your own price comes after the free survey, in writing, with any extras listed. That is the price you pay.

Does it heat as well as cool?

Yes. Almost every modern system does both, and many people use it as the main heating in rooms the radiators never quite reach.

Where does the outdoor unit go?

On wall brackets or on a stand at ground level, usually at the side or back of the house. We agree the spot and the pipe route with you at the survey.

Can the indoor unit go on an inside wall?

Often, yes. The water it collects then needs a small pump to carry it away, which adds to the price. The quote says so if it applies.

Will there be much mess?

One small hole through the wall for the pipework, trunking run neatly outside, and everything cleaned up before we leave.

How often does it need servicing?

Once a year is the usual advice for a home system. Busy shops and kitchens are better checked every six months.

Do I need planning permission?

It depends on the house and where the outdoor unit goes. We look at this at the survey and tell you before you commit to anything.
